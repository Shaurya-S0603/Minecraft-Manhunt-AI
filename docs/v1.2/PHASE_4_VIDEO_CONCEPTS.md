# Phase 4 — Three original Dream-style Manhunt video concepts

Record your own footage and display the real game results. The scripts below specify what to film, with alternate beats for either outcome. Narrator lines are optional prompts.

## 1. I Trained a Minecraft Hunter on My Escapes

**Hook.** Cold open on the hunter closing the gap. Freeze before the hit. “Yesterday this hunter spent the opening punching a tree. Today it has to decide whether to do that again.”

**Estimated runtime.** 13–15 minutes.

| Time | Scene and editing notes |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:35 | Start with a short pressure sequence. Cut between your runner camera and a verified hunter POV or spectator angle. Show the first-tag timer and leave the outcome unresolved. |
| 0:35–2:15 | Explain first-hit rules. Put **PRESS** and **GEAR** on screen with one sentence each. Show the runner lead and the tracking cooldown once. |
| 2:15–5:45 | Play two training rounds. Use cuts of one actual opening pickup or craft and one chase-heavy start. Let a failed chase stay in the montage, and pair each opening card with the logged result. |
| 5:45–7:00 | Freeze learning. Reveal the held-out route and compare the learned and baseline selections against the same scripted setup. Keep the number of completed rounds and tags on screen. |
| 7:00–12:30 | Play a human-runner final on a fresh route. Use a shore or climb as the midpoint reversal. In the final approach, switch to real-time audio and limit the overlay to timer and separation when observable. |
| 12:30–15:00 | Resolve the opening clip. If the hunter tags you, show the scoreboard and the specific route or resource choice that mattered. If you escape, show the route that cost it time. Recap what the small trial did and did not establish. |

**Key moments.** The first completed craft, the point at which held-out policy freezes, and the final tagged hit or survived timer.

**Featured mechanics.** Learned opening choice, native inventory events, cooldown-limited bearing, frozen baseline comparison.

## 2. Can My AI Hunter Follow Me Through the Nether?

**Hook.** “When I change dimensions, the hunter loses my direction. The only clue it keeps is a portal it actually saw.”

**Estimated runtime.** 15–18 minutes.

| Time | Scene and editing notes |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:45 | Show the portal glow, then cut to the last valid Overworld cue. Put **TRAIL LOST** on screen only when the logs show it. |
| 0:45–2:30 | Explain that lava bridging is enabled for this episode and requires real nonflammable blocks. Show the inventory count and introduce two possible entrances. Keep the route plan secret from the AI. |
| 2:30–6:00 | Lead the hunter across dry terrain, then use a river or climb to break sight. Show one genuine resupply decision and its effect on the gap. |
| 6:00–10:30 | Approach the two portal paths and enter one. Alternate runner footage with the hunter reaching an entrance it previously observed. If the real portal is unseen, show the hunter's stalled or expired trail and use a second take as an explicitly labeled repeat. |
| 10:30–15:30 | Build tension around a short lava lane with a flat far bank. Use one long take to show the hunter's inventory decreasing after each confirmed placement. If a crossing fails, leave the recovery in the edit. |
| 15:30–18:00 | Resolve with a tagged hit or runner survival. Replay the first portal observation and the actual dimension transition. |

**Key moments.** The last visible entrance, the first confirmed transition, and the last lava-bridge block or the moment the lane becomes unsafe.

**Featured mechanics.** Observed-portal memory, dimension-scoped tracking, nonflammable supplies, inventory-backed bridges.

## 3. Can My AI Hunter Cross the Ravine Before Time Runs Out?

**Hook.** From the far bank, pan down to the first block the Hunter has placed. “It has enough blocks to cross. I just need it to hesitate.”

**Estimated runtime.** 11–14 minutes.

| Time | Scene and editing notes |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | Open on a close approach on the far bank. Cut before the tag and show how much time is left. |
| 0:30–2:30 | Establish first-hit rules and the head start. Show the short ravine and a longer dry way around it. |
| 2:30–5:30 | Run a round in which you break sight before the crossing. Capture the Hunter's real pickups and bank approach from a separate camera. |
| 5:30–9:30 | Play the rematch on the same terrain. Show the verified block count as the Hunter places and steps onto each block. When the bridge fails, follow its recovery route. |
| 9:30–14:00 | Return to the opening shot. Let the confirmed tag or timer decide the ending. Compare the two rounds using first-tag time, placements and completed crossings. |

**Key moments.** The first bank approach, the far-bank step, and the last seconds before the tag or timeout.

**Featured mechanics.** Head starts, line-of-sight tracking, inventory-backed bridges, replay, and first-hit scoring.
